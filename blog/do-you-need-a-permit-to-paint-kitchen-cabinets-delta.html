Kitchen Cabinet Painting in Delta: Do You Need a Permit?

Painting is on the City of Delta's list of minor work that needs no building permit, and so are cupboards. Painting your existing kitchen cabinets in Delta, Ladner or Tsawwassen falls under that list. A permit question starts when the job grows into plumbing, wiring, gas or changes to the building.

By Texora Painting & Drywall  •  October 2026  •  5 min read

Kitchen cabinet painting in Delta, BC raises one practical question before any paint comes out: does the City need to hear about it? Delta's answer is on its Building & Renovating page, and this guide reads that page for a cabinet job. Delta has three communities, North Delta, Ladner and Tsawwassen, and the City's page covers all of them.

We are Texora Painting & Drywall, based at 1299 56 St in Tsawwassen. As kitchen cabinet painters in Delta, Ladner and Tsawwassen, we write from the painting side. This is not legal advice. Delta's page says its guidance is based solely on the information provided, so check your own project with the City.

What does the City of Delta's list say about painting and cupboards?

The page puts work that needs no building permit under the heading of minor modifications. Painting is on that list. So are cupboards, planters, partial height room dividers, roofing repair or replacement, and fences within the City's height rules. The same page says a building permit is required to alter, renovate or repair existing buildings or structures, to add to an existing building, and to complete an unfinished area such as a basement.

Plumbing, electrical and gas work have their own lines on that page. The table sets the common kitchen jobs against it.

CupboardsCupboards are listed as minor work that needs no building permit. The page does not say whether that covers building them, replacing them or both, so describe the job when you ask.

Does a cabinet painting job touch anything on the permit list?

The steps on our cabinet painting page are surface work. Doors and drawer fronts come off, every surface is degreased and sanded to remove the old sheen, an adhesion primer goes on, doors and drawer fronts are sprayed, frames are sprayed or brush-finished, and the hardware goes back on. None of those steps changes plumbing, wiring, gas or the structure of the building.

The Ladner Village kitchen shows the scope. The doors were removed, shop-painted and reinstalled with new soft-close hardware, and the hinges were adjusted for an even reveal. The Ladner Village kitchen cabinet repaint page has every step, and none of them touched the building.

Our cabinet painting page lists no plumbing, wiring or gas work, and our bathroom refresh page says plainly that it does not include plumbing or tile. If your plan includes that kind of work, treat it as a separate job with its own permit question.

When does a cabinet refresh turn into a permit job?

Mostly when the layout changes. Painting keeps every box where it is. Refacing swaps the doors and covers the face frames with veneer, and only new cabinets let you move the sink or change where the cabinets run, as our guide to painting vs refacing kitchen cabinets in Delta explains.

Moving a sink means altering plumbing, and Delta's page says that takes a plumbing permit. New lighting or outlets mean altering wiring or fixtures, which takes an electrical permit. Bigger changes to the building itself fall under the building-permit list above.

Settle the layout first. A kitchen that keeps its layout is a painting job with a short permit question, and one that changes it is worth a call to the City before anyone orders cabinets.

Who do you ask at the City of Delta?

The page names the Development Department's Application Centre, at 604-946-3380 or development@delta.ca. Describe the job in plain terms, for example paint the existing cabinets, same layout, no plumbing or wiring changes, because the City's guidance depends on what you tell it. For electrical and gas permits, Delta's page points to Technical Safety BC.

How does Delta take an application if you do need a permit?

For a single detached house or a duplex, including renovations, the City's How to Apply page says applications go through Delta Online, its online application system, with an Electronic Plan Submission Guide for instructions. The plan processing fee is paid through that submission, and payment instructions for the building permit fee arrive when the permit is ready to issue. Once an application is complete you get an email naming your Plans Examiner, their contact details and your file number.

The page does not list what a residential interior renovation needs. It says the requirements depend on the scope and nature of the proposal. Plumbing permit fees depend on the number and type of fixtures, according to the same page, so a sink move is priced differently from a full kitchen rebuild.

Does a house built before 1990 change the cabinet prep?

Lead is not a permit matter, but it belongs on the same checklist. Health Canada says a home built before 1960 probably contains lead-based paint, and that the exterior of a home built between 1960 and 1990 may contain it. Paint on interior surfaces from that period may also contain lead in smaller amounts.

Cabinet prep includes sanding, because the old sheen comes off before the adhesion primer goes on. Health Canada's advice is not to use sanders, heat guns or blowlamps to remove lead-based paint. It also says to keep children and pregnant women away from the work area, to cover doorways and vents with plastic sheeting and tape, and to find out what is on a surface by sending paint chips to a lab or hiring a contractor with x-ray equipment. Ask any painter how they handle suspect paint before the sanding starts. Tell us the year your house was built when you ask for a quote. We ask for it on older homes so the prep can be planned around it.

How long does a cabinet painting job take when no permit is involved?

When a job needs no permit, the schedule is the painter's. Our cabinet painting page says the written estimate sets out the schedule for removal, spraying, cure time and reinstallation, with doors spraying while frames cure. One Ladner Village kitchen took three days from doors off to doors reinstalled. Door count, material and condition vary from kitchen to kitchen, so treat three days as one example and ask for your own schedule in writing.

If the walls around the cabinets are getting paint too, ask for both on one quote. Our interior painting page covers walls, ceilings and trim.
