Premium Finishes for a Premium Community

Tsawwassen Springs is a master-planned community with newer homes built mostly post-2015. The construction quality is high — stucco and Hardie board exteriors, modern open-concept interiors — and the expectations of homeowners here match. This isn't the neighbourhood for rushed work or cheap products. Springs residents notice the difference between a careful painter and one who cuts corners on prep or product selection.

We've worked in Tsawwassen Springs since the community filled out, and we understand its specific requirements. On the exterior, stucco needs proper elastomeric products that flex with the substrate and stay watertight over BC winters. Hardie board responds best to high-adhesion primers and breathable topcoats. On the interior, Springs homes often feature high ceilings, feature walls, and trim-heavy millwork that requires the kind of steady brush work that separates finishing painters from general contractors.

Because Texora Painting is based in Delta, we're always nearby. Same-day quotes, fast scheduling, and a team that communicates throughout the job — that's what Springs homeowners tell us they value most.

A small subdivision built around the golf course

Tsawwassen Springs sits within the Tsawwassen Golf & Country Club, a newer subdivision built on and around the golf course itself rather than a standalone tract of land. As noted above, the community was built out mostly after 2015, which is worth knowing before a repaint: a newer-build exterior has a different starting point than an older Tsawwassen property with decades of past paint layers and prior repairs to account for.

What to watch for on newer stucco and Hardie board

Builder-grade exterior coatings on a newer home often hold up reasonably well for the first several years. Hairline stucco cracking from normal settling can still show up earlier than expected, and it's worth having it addressed as soon as it's noticed rather than waiting for a full repaint, since a small settling crack that lets moisture behind the stucco can turn a quick caulk-and-fill job into a bigger repair later. Hardie board itself tends to be stable, but the caulked joints between panels are usually the first thing to fail, so it's worth asking whether those joints are being inspected and resealed as part of a repaint, not just painted over.

Getting strata colour approval right

If your unit or the exterior of your home falls under a strata council in Tsawwassen Springs, plan for the approval step before you plan a painting date. Most councils want a written request with the specific paint product, colour name, and sheen, sometimes alongside a physical sample or paint chip. It's worth asking whether a painter can provide colour samples ahead of a quote specifically for this purpose, since waiting until after the estimate to start that conversation is a common reason projects get delayed. Once approval comes through, the painting timeline itself doesn't usually change from a non-strata property.

Interior trim and feature walls in newer homes

Open-concept layouts and higher ceilings, common in newer floor plans, change how interior painting gets approached. Long, uninterrupted trim runs need a steady hand when cutting in, since any wobble in the line is far more visible against a long wall than a short one. Feature walls, which show up often in these newer layouts, typically get painted after the surrounding walls are finished and fully dry, so the tape line sits on a cured surface rather than one that's still soft. Lighting matters more than people expect on a feature wall too: a colour that reads as a rich accent under warm evening light can look flat and grey under daylight from a large window, so it's worth viewing swatches at different times of day before committing to a shade.

What to ask for in a newer-build quote

  • Whether strata colour approval is something you need to arrange before scheduling, and how long your council typically takes
  • Whether Hardie board caulk joints are being inspected and resealed as part of the job, not just painted over
  • How feature walls and accent colours are handled if you're not fully set on a shade yet
  • Whether trim, doors, and baseboards are quoted separately from wall painting

What a full interior repaint typically includes

A full interior repaint typically covers walls, and sometimes ceilings and trim as separate line items depending on the quote. It's worth confirming whether ceilings are included by default or priced separately, since a lot of homeowners assume they are and find out otherwise partway through. Furniture and flooring protection, patching small nail holes and dings, and a final walkthrough to catch any missed spots should all be part of the scope, not extras that show up on a final invoice.

Protecting new flooring and finishes

Newer homes often have finishes worth extra care during a paint job: engineered hardwood or luxury vinyl flooring, quartz counters, and built-in millwork are all more sensitive to solvents and accidental drips than older, more worn surfaces. Floor protection should go down before any prep work starts, not just before painting begins, since sanding and patching create as much mess as the paint itself.

Paint durability and touch-ups

High-traffic areas like hallways, stairwells, and kitchens show wear faster than a quiet bedroom, so it's worth asking whether a more washable, higher-durability finish is being used in those spots specifically rather than the same sheen throughout the house. Keeping leftover paint, labelled with the room and date, makes future touch-ups far easier to match, since paint formulas and even can-to-can batches can shift slightly over time.

Ventilation during an interior job

Fresh paint releases odour and some VOCs as it dries, more with oil-based products than water-based ones, so it's worth asking how a crew plans to ventilate the space during and after painting, especially in a newer, more airtight home where mechanical ventilation may need to run longer than usual to clear the smell.
