Brick staining in Delta, Tsawwassen, Ladner and Metro Vancouver

Brick staining soaks into the brick rather than sitting on top, so it refreshes the colour while keeping the natural texture and depth. Brick covered in ordinary paint reads as fake and can trap moisture, which is why a lot of homeowners hold off on updating theirs.

Across Delta, Tsawwassen, Ladner and the wider Metro Vancouver area, many homes built through the 1970s, '80s and '90s have orange, red or pink-toned brick that now feels dated next to a modern exterior. If your brick clashes with new siding, a fresh paint colour, or the look you're going for, staining changes the colour without hiding what's underneath.

We read the brick's porosity, test colour on the actual surface, walk you through the options and set clear expectations before we start.

What is efflorescence, and why does it matter before staining brick?

Efflorescence is a white, chalky mineral deposit left behind as moisture moves up through brick and evaporates, most often near grade or where water sits against the wall. It has to come off with the right masonry cleaner, a thorough rinse and full drying time before any stain goes on, or the salts get trapped and the colour turns patchy within a season.

Why stain brick instead of painting it?

It stays breathable

Stain penetrates the brick and lets it breathe, so moisture isn't trapped behind a film the way it can be with ordinary paint, which matters on the rainy South Coast.

It won't peel like paint

Because it soaks in rather than sitting on the surface, a proper brick stain doesn't blister and peel off in sheets the way painted brick eventually does.

It keeps the texture

You still see and feel the brick: the grain, the mortar lines, the subtle colour variation. It looks refreshed.

A natural, high-end look

The goal is a finish that looks like the brick was always this colour.

What brick staining can do for a Delta home

Modernize a dated exterior

Take orange or red '80s brick to a soft white, warm grey, greige or deep charcoal that fits a modern Metro Vancouver home.

Tie the whole exterior together

Match or complement new siding, trim and a freshly painted front door so the brick stops fighting the rest of the house.

Refresh interior brick and fireplaces

Dated interior brick and fireplace surrounds can be stained for a lighter, current look while keeping the brick's character.

Colour options and expectations

We help you choose a colour and, just as importantly, set realistic expectations. Brick colour, porosity and existing tone all affect the final result, which is why we test first.

Why does a brick stain test patch matter?

Colour, mineral content and porosity vary from batch to batch, sometimes brick to brick on the same wall, so the same stain reads differently in different spots. Check a test patch in daylight and in shade, after it has fully cured, and more than once before the whole house is committed to that colour.

How does stain differ from paint on brick?

Brick is porous, and moisture moves through it constantly, in from rain and out again as it dries. A film like ordinary paint blocks that movement, and the trapped moisture is what makes painted brick blister and peel, and in freeze-thaw conditions can flake the brick face off, a problem called spalling. A penetrating stain soaks into the pores instead, so the wall keeps breathing and there's no film to peel.

What if the brick has already been painted?

A true stain needs porous, unsealed masonry to soak into. If the brick has been painted at some point in the past, that paint film sits between the stain and the brick itself, and the stain can't penetrate the way it's meant to. In that situation the existing paint usually needs to be stripped or otherwise dealt with first, or a different product is the more honest option than staining over a coating and calling it a stain.

Questions worth asking before you book a brick staining quote

  • Has the brick been cleaned and checked for efflorescence, and is that included in the quote?
  • Will a test patch be applied and left to cure before the full job starts?
  • Is the product a penetrating stain, or a film-forming coating being sold as one?
  • How is mortar handled, will it be masked or does the colour carry over the joints?
  • What's the plan if the test patch colour isn't quite right on your specific brick?
