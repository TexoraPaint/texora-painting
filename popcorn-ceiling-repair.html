What is popcorn ceiling texture?

Popcorn ceiling (also called acoustic ceiling texture, cottage cheese ceiling, or stucco ceiling) was one of the most widely used ceiling finishes in North American homes from the 1960s through the early 1990s. In Tsawwassen and Ladner, where significant residential development occurred in the 1970s and 1980s, popcorn ceilings are extremely common.

Popcorn texture traps dust, is nearly impossible to clean, shows water stains prominently and makes rooms feel lower and darker. We remove popcorn ceilings throughout Delta.

Our interior painting service can cover the room once the ceiling work is complete.

Should you patch or remove a popcorn ceiling?

Patch repair

Best for: small areas of physical damage (a water stain, a section knocked by furniture, a hole from a fixture). We spray-match the existing texture and blend the patch into the surrounding area.

Aged popcorn settles and fades, so an exact match is rare, but we get as close as possible with acoustic spray compound. If you are not planning to repaint the whole ceiling, a patch repair is the most practical option.

Full removal

Best for: modernizing your home, preparing for sale, removing heavily damaged or stained popcorn, or addressing asbestos concerns. Full removal gives you a clean slate: smooth, skim-coated ceilings that look like new construction.

Signs your popcorn ceiling needs attention in Delta

A water stain, a section knocked by furniture or a hole left by a fixture is best handled as a patch, matched with acoustic spray compound. Heavy damage or staining, damage in more than one room, or a ceiling that traps dust and makes the room feel lower and darker is where full removal and a smooth, skim-coated finish make sense.

How popcorn ceiling removal runs in Delta

1. Asbestos testing (pre-1990 homes)

For homes built before 1990, testing is mandatory before we begin. We can recommend certified asbestos testing services in Delta.

Results come back from the testing service before we schedule the work. If asbestos is found, a licensed abatement contractor must remove the material before we can proceed.

2. Furniture protection and prep

Removal is wet and messy, so floors, furniture and every other surface go under plastic sheeting before the first scrape.

3. Moisten and scrape

The ceiling is lightly misted with water to soften the texture. Once the texture has absorbed enough moisture to release (usually 15–20 minutes), it is scraped cleanly from the drywall below using wide scrapers. Care is taken not to gouge the drywall face, which would require additional repair work.

4. Skim coat

After scraping, the bare drywall surface has minor imperfections, screw dimples, and slight texture from the old material. We apply one to two coats of skim coat compound to create a smooth, flat surface.

5. Prime and paint

The skim-coated ceiling is primed with drywall primer and painted in your chosen colour and sheen. Flat paint is the standard for ceilings because it hides any minor surface irregularities better than any other sheen level.

Asbestos in pre-1990 homes

Popcorn ceiling texture applied before 1990 in BC may contain chrysotile asbestos. This was a common additive in spray-texture products of that era. Homes in Tsawwassen and Ladner built between the 1960s and late 1980s are at particular risk.

Disturbing asbestos-containing material without testing and proper abatement is illegal under WorkSafeBC regulations and dangerous to your health. Before any popcorn ceiling removal, have the material tested by a certified asbestos inspector. If asbestos is present, a licensed abatement contractor must remove it, and we proceed only after clearance is confirmed, since we do not work on confirmed asbestos-containing materials.

Popcorn ceiling questions from Delta homeowners

Popcorn texture applied before 1990 in BC frequently contained chrysotile asbestos, which was added to the spray compound for durability and fire resistance. In Tsawwassen and Ladner, where many homes were built in the 1970s and 1980s, this is a very real concern. Only a test by a certified asbestos professional tells you for certain, and the texture should not be disturbed before that test, since even minor scraping can release fibres.

After confirming no asbestos is present (or completing abatement if it is), the ceiling is misted with water to soften the texture, then scraped clean with wide scrapers. The bare drywall is then skim-coated one to two times to create a smooth surface, sanded, primed, and painted. The scraping phase generates significant debris, so thorough room protection is essential.

Removal and skim coat runs approximately $2–$4 per square foot of ceiling area. A 200 sq ft room is roughly $400–$800 for removal and skim coat.

Painting is additional. For asbestos-containing ceilings, licensed abatement is an added cost that varies depending on the contractor and extent of the work, typically $500–$2,500 for a residential room.

A single room gets removal and a first skim coat, then a final skim coat, sanding and priming once that's dry, with painting after the primer dries. Your written estimate sets out the schedule for your rooms.
