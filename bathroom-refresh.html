Refresh Your Bathroom — Paint, Drywall & Vanity

Not every bathroom needs a full gut-renovation. Often a fresh, professional paint job, some drywall repair, and a refinished vanity completely transform the space — for a fraction of the cost and disruption. That's exactly what Texora Painting specializes in for homeowners across Tsawwassen, Ladner, and Delta.

Bathrooms are humid, so they need the right approach: mould-resistant, moisture-tolerant paints, proper prep, and repair of any water-damaged drywall before finishing. We also refinish vanities and cabinets with a durable sprayed finish — see our cabinet painting service.

Please note: we focus on the paint, drywall, and cabinet side of bathroom updates — we don't do plumbing or tile work. If that's all you need to refresh your space, we're a perfect, cost-effective fit. Free quotes, always.

Why bathrooms need a different approach

Bathrooms run through more humidity cycles in a week than almost any other room in the house. Every shower sends warm, moist air against the walls and ceiling, and that moisture is what standard wall paint isn't built to handle. Over time it shows up as peeling near the tub surround, or a sheen that's gone dull and won't wipe clean no matter how hard you scrub. In corners where air barely moves, that same moisture tends to show up as dark speckling instead. Moisture- and mould-resistant paints are formulated to shed that humidity instead of absorbing it, which is why we use them specifically in bathrooms rather than whatever's left over from the rest of the house.

The exhaust fan does more for your paint job than most people realize. A fan that's undersized, blocked, or rarely switched on lets steam sit on the walls and ceiling far longer than it should, and that's often the real reason a bathroom repaint fails early even when the prep was solid. If your fan isn't pulling air the way it should, it's worth having that looked at around the same time as a repaint, since a fresh coat over a room that still can't clear its own humidity is only a short-term fix.

What's worth checking before painting a bathroom

Soft spots around the tub, shower, and under the vanity are usually where a slow leak or splashing has already gotten into the drywall, so that's worth a look before anyone opens a can of paint. Painting over active mildew rather than treating it first just traps it under the new coat, where it keeps growing out of sight. Cracked or gapped caulk at the tub, sink, and baseboard is also worth checking, since it's a common way moisture gets behind the wall in the first place, and it's a cheap thing to fix compared to what happens if it's left.

Water-damaged drywall: what the repair actually involves

As already noted above, repairing water-damaged or mouldy drywall is one of our core services: we remove and replace or patch the affected drywall, finish it paint-ready, and repaint to match. The section that's affected generally has to come out rather than get painted over, whether the damage came from a slow leak, a gap in the seal around the tub, or ongoing humidity. See our water-damage drywall repair page for more detail, and our texture matching page if the repaired section needs to blend with a textured surface around it.

Vanity and cabinet refinishing

A dated vanity is often the single biggest thing holding a bathroom back visually, and it's rarely worth replacing just for the finish. As the intro above notes, we refinish vanities and cabinets with a durable sprayed finish, the same process covered in full on our cabinet painting page: doors and drawer fronts removed, degreased, sanded, and primed before the topcoat goes on.

Ceiling and trim in a small, humid room

Bathroom ceilings take on more humidity than most rooms in the house, so the same moisture-resistant thinking that applies to the walls carries up there too. Trim and door casings in a bathroom are covered in more detail on our trim, doors and ceilings page, including the sheen and dry-time guidance that applies just as much to a bathroom door as anywhere else in the home.

What to decide before your quote

  • Whether the project needs drywall repair or just paint
  • Vanity or cabinet refinishing, included or not
  • Colour direction for a small, mostly-tiled room
  • Whether the exhaust fan needs a separate look (outside our scope, but worth flagging)
  • Timeline, especially if it's the only bathroom in the house

What we don't do

Our side of a bathroom refresh stops at paint, drywall, and cabinet refinishing. If a project needs new tile, a different layout, or plumbing work, that's outside what we do, and we'll say so plainly rather than take on a scope we're not set up for. For a lot of bathrooms, though, paint, a drywall repair, and a refinished vanity solve the actual problem without needing any of that.

The warranty in plain terms

Bathroom projects carry the same written estimate and workmanship warranty as any other job at Texora, one to three years depending on scope. That covers paint that fails from how it was applied, drywall tape or patches that fail, or caulking we installed that lets go. It doesn't cover moisture getting in from a source outside our work, like a leaking fixture or failed grout, or normal wear from daily use. There's a final walkthrough before any project is called finished, so you get a chance to flag anything in scope before we consider the job done.
