How a drywall installation job runs in Port Coquitlam

1

Site visit and quote

We assess the framing, measure the space, identify board type requirements, and provide a written quote covering all labour and materials for your PoCo project.

2

Board selection and hanging

Right board for every location (moisture-resistant, fire-rated, or standard), cut precisely and screwed firmly to studs or ceiling joists at the correct spacing.

3

Tape, mud and sand

All joints and screw holes taped and mudded in three coats with full drying and sanding between each. Corner bead installed and finished on all outside corners.

4

Level 4 finish and prime

Final sand to a Level 4 (or Level 5 on request) standard. Primer coat applied, so walls are ready for your paint colour the day we complete the job.

Board type

Bathrooms, laundry rooms and walls against below-grade concrete need moisture-resistant board, and furnace rooms and other code-required fire separations need fire-rated Type X. Standard board does the rest, so one basement often uses two or three board types.

What a finish level means for your paint job

Level 4, three coats of compound over joints and screws sanded smooth, is the standard and holds up under eggshell or satin in normal light. Level 5 adds a skim coat over the whole surface, worth it under flat paint, light colours or low-angle window light.

Living upstairs while the basement gets finished

Ask how the noisy, dusty stages will be phased through the day, how the stairwell opening will be sealed (plastic sheeting and a zipper door are common) and whether the sanders have dust extraction. The aim is a usable house in the evenings and on weekends.

What to ask for in an installation quote

A clear installation quote should break down the board type for each area, the finish level you're getting (Level 4 versus Level 5), whether painting is included or priced separately, and a realistic timeline with drying time built in between coats rather than compressed to look faster on paper. It's also worth asking who handles corner bead and outside corners specifically, since a sloppy outside corner is one of the more visible mistakes on an otherwise good drywall job.
