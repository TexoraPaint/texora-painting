What Ladner Village heritage homes need before a quote

Multiple paint layers, original wood siding, and older trim profiles all affect how much prep a job needs. If a home falls into the pre-1990 range, ask whether lead paint is a consideration: Health Canada advises against sanding or heat-stripping old paint that might contain it, and any contractor working on a home that age should have a plan for that.

Choosing exterior colours for a Ladner Village heritage home

Heritage-era homes generally read best with a body colour, a trim colour, and sometimes a third accent for the door or window sashes, rather than one flat colour across everything. A common approach is picking two or three colours that complement the roofline and any masonry or foundation colour that isn't changing, then testing sample patches on the actual wall in different light before choosing, since a colour in morning shade can read very differently once the afternoon sun is on it.

Trim, door and ceiling painting in Ladner Village

Trim styles from the 1930s and earlier, like deeper window casings and panel doors, generally take longer to cut in by hand than a modern flat-profile trim package would, since every groove and edge needs individual attention rather than a quick pass with a roller. Trim, door, and ceiling painting quotes should reflect that difference in labour, so ask how a contractor accounts for period trim detail when pricing the work.

Cabinet painting in Ladner Village kitchens

Cabinet painting starts with a full cleaning to remove cooking grease and grime, since paint won't bond properly over it, followed by light sanding so the primer has something to grip. Ask whether doors and drawer fronts will be removed and sprayed flat, or painted in place.

What to expect from a quote visit in Ladner Village

Expect a quote visit for a character home to take longer than for a newer build, since there's more to assess: siding condition, trim detail, and access around older windows or original hardware. Walk the exterior with the contractor and have them point out anything that might affect the scope, like signs of past water intrusion or wood rot, before they finalize a written estimate.

Questions to ask a painter about a Ladner heritage home

Not every painting company has experience with older wood siding and original trim profiles, or with lead-safe prep practices, so it's worth asking directly. Start with photos of comparable heritage or older-home projects, then how they identify sound versus failing wood before quoting a final price.

Comparing quotes for a Ladner Village character home

It's worth getting more than one quote for a character home, since assessments of paint condition and prep scope can vary between contractors. Compare what's included along with the bottom line: whether the quote calls out caulking and re-glazing separately, which sections it notes as needing extra primer, and how many finish coats are planned.

Plaster walls in older Ladner homes

Hairline cracking is a normal part of how plaster ages, and a patched section needs a slightly different primer approach to blend with the surrounding texture than a standard drywall patch would. If your home has plaster walls, mention it when booking a quote, since it can change both the prep time and the products used.

Our warranty on Ladner Village jobs

Every job, heritage or new construction, comes with a workmanship warranty of one to three years depending on the service and scope, as stated on your estimate. It covers issues that trace back to prep or application, like peeling, blistering, or caulking failure, but it doesn't cover normal wear, pre-existing coatings, or moisture problems coming from the building envelope itself. If something in the approved scope needs attention, call us first so it can be assessed and corrected under warranty before anyone else touches the area.
