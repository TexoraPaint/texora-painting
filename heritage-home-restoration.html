Heritage & Character Home Restoration in Delta & Metro Vancouver

Heritage homes deserve more than a generic "house painting" job. They often carry older materials, historic trim profiles, layers of decades-old coatings, and fine architectural details that reward patience and skill over speed. Rushed prep or the wrong approach can permanently damage original features that can't be replaced.

From the character homes of Ladner Village and older Tsawwassen and Delta neighbourhoods to century and mid-century houses across Vancouver, Burnaby and New Westminster, Metro Vancouver is full of homes with real history. Our heritage restoration service is built for owners who want to preserve that character while improving the finish, without painting over what makes the home special.

We take a careful, informed approach to preparation and application, and we communicate clearly so you understand what we're doing and why at each stage. If your home is part of your identity and your legacy, you deserve painters who treat it that way too.

What Health Canada says about lead paint in older homes

Health Canada's guidance is direct about this: homes built before 1960 probably contain lead-based paint, and homes built between 1960 and 1990 may have it on the exterior, with the interior sometimes containing smaller amounts that can still be harmful to young children. Homes built after 1990 generally should not have lead-based paint. The guidance is also clear about what not to do to a suspected lead surface: don't sand it, don't use a heat gun on it, and don't use a blowlamp, since all three release lead into the air. Where a lead surface has to be worked around rather than disturbed, isolating the area with plastic sheeting over doorways and vents is the recommended precaution (Health Canada, lead-based paint).

How We Approach an Older Home

Patience Over Speed

Heritage prep can't be rushed. We take the time to assess layered coatings, fragile trim and original wood, and work in a way that protects the details.

Preserving Original Detail

Dentil trim, corbels, gable brackets, mouldings and profile work are what make a heritage home. We prep and coat them carefully to keep their crispness.

Lead-Safe Practices

Many older Metro Vancouver homes have lead-based coatings under later layers. Where that's a factor, we follow lead-safe procedures before disturbing existing paint.

Exterior & Interior Heritage Work

Siding, trim, fascia, porches and railings outside; plaster walls, wood trim, doors and mouldings inside, all restored with the same care.

Restoration, Not Just a Repaint

Repair Before Finish

Rotted trim, split wood and failed caulking are repaired first, so the finish restores the home rather than just covering problems.

The Right Products

Old plaster is not drywall

Plaster walls behave differently than drywall, and homes built with lath and plaster often develop fine, hairline cracks over the decades as the house settles and the plaster ages, which is a normal part of an old building rather than a sign of a structural problem. Patching plaster properly usually calls for a bonding agent so new material actually adheres to the old, and wider cracks typically need mesh reinforcement rather than just being filled over, or the crack tends to telegraph straight back through the next coat of paint. If a contractor is planning to patch plaster the same way they'd patch drywall, it's worth asking why, since the two materials don't behave the same way under a fresh coat.

Matching an original trim profile

When historic trim is damaged beyond repair, replacing it is more involved than a trip to the hardware store. Off-the-shelf mouldings rarely match the exact profile that was milled decades ago, so a section that needs full replacement sometimes calls for a custom-run piece to match the original shape, rather than a close substitute that stands out once it's painted. It's worth asking, before any trim comes off, whether the plan is to match the existing profile or replace it with something similar, and what that means for cost and timeline.

Ladner's history

Ladner, one of Delta's three communities, is described on Wikipedia as "a 19th-century fishing village in northwest Delta." It's background worth knowing if you're restoring a character home in that part of the area.

Questions worth asking before restoring a character home

  • Has lead paint been considered on a pre-1990 home, and what precautions are planned if it's found?
  • Is damaged trim being matched to the original profile, or replaced with something similar?
  • How will cracked plaster be patched, and is a bonding agent or mesh part of the plan?
  • What happens to the timeline if hidden damage turns up once work starts?
