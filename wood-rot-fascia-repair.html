How is wood rot found before it spreads?

The standard check is probing suspect wood with an awl or screwdriver at fascia edges, board ends, corner and peak joints, window sills, under drip edges, behind gutters and anywhere caulking has cracked, and if the tip sinks in without resistance, that section is compromised. Rot usually starts as a soft spot under paint that still looks fine.

Signs of wood rot on a Delta house

  • Paint that's bubbling, cracking in a grid pattern, or peeling in sheets along fascia or trim
  • A soft or spongy feel when you press on a windowsill, corner board, or fascia edge
  • Discoloured or darkened wood, especially near joints and corners
  • Gaps opening up where caulking has shrunk or pulled away from a seam
  • Gutters that overflow, sag, or drip onto the fascia below instead of into the downspout

Should rotten wood be filled or replaced?

A small area of soft wood near the surface, with sound wood underneath, can usually be dug out and rebuilt with an epoxy wood filler or a two-part consolidant, then sanded and shaped to match the original profile. Once rot has worked through more of a board's thickness or travelled along the grain, filling is only a short-term fix, and the damaged length gets cut out and spliced, or the board replaced.

Why prime every side of repaired wood?

New or repaired wood needs primer on every surface before it goes back on the house, including the ends and back that won't show. End grain pulls in moisture far faster than the flat face, so an unprimed cut end is often where the next round of rot starts.

Why does the water source have to be fixed first?

Clogged or sagging gutters, missing flashing, failed caulking at a joint, or a downspout dumping water against a corner are the usual reasons fascia rots. Replacing the wood without fixing the water usually means paying for the same repair again.

What does the wood repair warranty cover?

Every job gets a final walkthrough, and if anything in the approved scope doesn't hold up to our own standard, we fix it as part of our satisfaction guarantee. Beyond that, our workmanship warranty runs one, two, or three years depending on the service and scope, and it's spelled out on your written estimate.

It covers failures caused by our own workmanship, things like peeling, blistering, or adhesion loss from improper prep or application. It does not cover new rot or moisture intrusion that shows up after the fact, normal wear and fading, building-envelope problems, or damage from something else entirely, like a gutter that fails two years later and lets water back in.
