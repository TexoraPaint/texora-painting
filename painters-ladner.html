What to ask for in a painting quote

A good painting quote should tell you more than a total number. Before you sign anything, ask what surface prep is included: scraping, sanding, patching nail holes, and caulking gaps around trim and windows. Ask how many coats are planned, and whether that's primer plus finish coats or just finish coats over old paint. Ask what brand and product line will be used, since not every exterior paint is rated for the same conditions. And ask what happens if it rains partway through an exterior job, since a coating applied to a damp surface won't cure properly and may need to be redone.

A written estimate protects both sides. It should list the rooms or surfaces covered, the paint brand and sheen, the number of coats, and what is and isn't included, like ceiling touch-ups or crown moulding. If a quote is vague on any of these points, it's worth asking before work starts rather than after.

Questions worth asking about the process

A typical job order looks similar across most professional painting companies: protect the space, prep the surface, prime where needed, then apply finish coats. It's worth asking exactly how each stage is handled on your project rather than assuming. Ask whether furniture and floors will be covered or moved before prep starts. Ask whether bare wood, drywall patches, or repaired spots get a separate coat of primer before the topcoat, since skipping that step is a known way for patched areas to end up with a slightly different sheen later. Ask how many finish coats are included and what happens if a section needs an extra coat for full, even coverage.

Dry time is also worth asking about, especially on a tight schedule. Paint chemistry sets its own pace: most interior latex products are dry to the touch within a few hours, but the manufacturer's full cure time, when the film can handle scrubbing or furniture pushed against it, is usually measured in weeks rather than hours. Exterior paints work the same way, and every manufacturer publishes minimum recoat windows. Applying a second coat before the first has properly set is a well-known way for an otherwise good paint job to fail early.

Choosing paint sheen for different rooms

Sheen affects both looks and durability, and it's worth understanding before you settle on a finish. Flat and matte finishes hide wall imperfections well but are harder to wipe clean, which is why they're most often used on ceilings and low-traffic rooms. Eggshell and satin sit in the middle: enough sheen to wipe down without highlighting every roller mark or seam. Semi-gloss and gloss are the most washable, which is why they're the usual choice for trim, doors, and cabinets, since those surfaces get touched the most. Ask which sheen is planned for which surface and why, since the right choice depends on how a room gets used, not just personal taste.

Drywall problems worth flagging before you paint

Hairline cracks around door and window frames, nail pops where a fastener has worked slightly loose from the framing, and seams that show through paint under strong light are common drywall issues in general, not specific to any one kind of home. Water staining is different: it's worth asking a contractor to check for the source before repainting over it, since paint alone won't fix a leak that's still active. Drywall repair before painting is almost always cheaper than dealing with the same crack again a year later.

Our warranty, in plain terms

Every job comes with a workmanship warranty, and the length depends on the service and scope, as stated on your estimate. It covers failures that trace back to our workmanship: peeling, blistering, or adhesion loss caused by improper prep or application, failed drywall tape or patches, caulking failure, or carpentry we installed coming loose. It doesn't cover normal wear and fading, moisture intrusion from the building envelope, settlement, pre-existing coatings, rot, mold or mildew, abuse, weather events, or work done by someone else. If something in the approved scope needs attention, call us before anyone else touches the area, so we can assess and correct it under warranty. We also do a final walkthrough on every job, and any workmanship deficiency found in the approved scope gets fixed before we consider the job complete.
